Inputs
bundled US states (simplify=1000), population; Michigan for the multipolygon modes.

New noncontiguous_cartogram module with create_noncontiguous_cartogram(gdf, column): every region keeps its shape and is scaled about its centroid to an area proportional to its value. reference sets the density (densest, largest, or a number), max_scale limits growth, multipart scales a multipolygon as a group or by part. The result has plot(style=) over the original outlines.

make_figures.py draws the figures on the PR branch.

Reference density

The default is the only reference under which every area is proportional without growth; for US population it makes most states very small. A float reference or reference="largest" with the cap gives a more readable map at the price of capped states.

Michigan (111 parts) at a scale of 0.5. "group" (default) is a smaller copy of the whole state, so the upper peninsula stays at its distance in proportion; "parts" keeps each part at its place and the gap between the peninsulas grows relative to them.

reference_region set to New Jersey: only DC is denser than the reference. Middle: DC is kept at its size (smaller than proportional); right: DC grows to 3.0 times its linear size. The median scale is 0.30 (0.10 with the default) and 16 states are below 5 % of their area (43 with the default). Regions are drawn largest first, so the grown DC lies under its neighbors.

US states by population. Left: the default reference (the densest region, DC): nothing grows, every area is proportional to the population, and 43 of 49 states are below 5 % of their area. Middle: the reference of the region with the largest value (California) with growth capped at the original size: the 11 denser states keep their original size and are smaller than proportional to their population, and the other states are scaled relative to California. Right: the same reference without the cap: New Jersey grows to 4.6 times its area and DC to 41.6 times.

The default reference colored by region in classic, default and dark: the reference fill of the style for the original outlines.
