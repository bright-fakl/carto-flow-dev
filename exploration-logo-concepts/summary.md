**Outcome (2026-10-27).** The design was the tall blob `t1` with gaps crossing at 72 degrees (vertical gap +10 degrees, horizontal -8 degrees), amber on the two right-hand regions, and was merged as the project logo and branding assets in PR 187 (main @ a126c0d). Light amber was darkened to `#c27400` for 3:1 contrast on white.

`marks.py <out_dir>` writes `<concept>-light.svg` and `<concept>-dark.svg` for each concept (committed in `svg/`); `fig.py` draws the legibility figure. The marks are plain geometry, so they carry no licensing constraint from fonts or third-party artwork (original work, covered by the project's MIT license).

## Concepts

| Concept | Idea | At 16 px | In one color | Covers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warped grid | A 3x3 grid bent around a centre cell that has grown to its data value | Frame and centre cell hold; interior lines blur | Frame plus a filled centre cell stays distinct | Flow cartogram (the core algorithm), and by extension the area-rebalancing idea of the others |
| Hexagon tiles | Seven hexagons of different sizes, the centre one amber | A rosette; the size differences are lost | Becomes a blob | Symbol and tile cartograms (mosaic layout) |
| Filled warped grid | The same warp with the cells filled as a choropleth (three tints), centre cell amber and grown | Reads as a data tile; centre and tints survive | Tints collapse to one color; centre cell still distinct | Cartogram plus thematic map (the library draws choropleths) |
| Half regular, half warped | The grid regular on the left and warped on the right, a grown amber cell on the right | Amber cell is a few pixels, the idea is lost | Works | The morph in one frame; weaker as a mark |
| Streamlines | Horizontal lines of the flow field parting around an amber region | Lines blur into a texture | Works at 32 px | The flow field of the algorithm; no grid, so less recognizable as a cartogram |
| Dorling circles | Five touching circles of different size, one amber | Holds well | Works | Dorling and symbol cartograms; the most generic of the four |
| Blob, four regions (`blob`) | A country-like blob (radial harmonics, no real country) cut by one wavy vertical and one wavy horizontal gap in the background color (the gaps are real gaps, so the mark works on any surface); three blue tints and one amber region | Four regions still separate; gaps close to about one pixel | Works with tints dropped | Flow cartogram on a country, regions of unequal size |
| Blob, stronger waves (`blob_wavy`) | Same with larger waves and a single blue | Same | Works | Same; simpler |
| Morph | Three nested outlines going from a rounded square to an amber circle | The circle holds; outlines vanish | Works | Morphing in general; no direct link to maps or areas |

## Observations

- The warped grid is the most specific to the library (a regular grid distorted to rebalance areas) and keeps its structure down to 16 px because the frame is the outer shape.
- Of the four added concepts the filled warped grid is the strongest: it keeps the frame of the plain grid, adds the idea of data (tints) and holds at 16 px. The streamlines are the most distinctive and tie to the flow algorithm, but they need a larger minimum size. The half-and-half grid does not read, and the circles are generic.
- The blob reads immediately as regions of a country and the unequal pieces suggest rebalanced areas, which the grid marks only imply. It is less abstract than the grid, so it is more recognizable at 64 px and above. Its weak point is 16 px, where the gaps are one pixel wide and the shape is a lump; the favicon would need thicker gaps and fewer wiggles. Whether the blob looks like a specific territory is a matter of taste (the current outline is a roundish shape with three harmonics).
- The hexagon mark is attractive at large sizes but at 16 px reads as a flower; it is a candidate for a secondary mark (symbol cartogram docs section) rather than the project logo.
- The morph mark is generic and its stacked offsets look off-centre at small sizes; not recommended.
- No wordmark is drawn yet. The 16 px favicon should be a simplified variant (frame plus centre cell, two interior lines), not the 128 px mark scaled down.

## Review of the blob (2026-10-26)

`blob_wavy` is the preferred design. The horizontal gap has a lower spatial frequency, and `blob_small` is the favicon variant with thicker gaps.

**Resemblance to the Windows logo.** The Windows mark is four equal, axis-aligned squares in four colors, separated by a straight cross of gaps. The blob shares the idea of four parts separated by a cross, and that is the risk. It is smaller when the following hold, and larger when they do not:

- The outline is an irregular blob, not a square, so the silhouette differs.
- The regions are unequal and the gaps wavy and off-center. `blob_small` is the weak case: at 16 px its gentle waves are nearly straight, and a small blob with a straight cross looks like a window pane. It should keep visibly curved gaps even at the cost of a little legibility, or use only one gap of 8 px plus one thinner.
- One accent region and a single blue, not four colors. The four-color variants of the first round should not be used.

This is a design judgment, not a trademark opinion; if the logo will be used widely, a check by someone qualified is worth doing before adoption. A cheap further separation is to tilt the cross by about 10 to 15 degrees so the gaps are not horizontal and vertical.

## Tilted cross and curvier favicon (2026-10-26)

`marks.py` now has `blob_tilt` and `blob_small_tilt` (the cut is rotated by 13 degrees about the blob center).

- Rotating the cross removes the horizontal and vertical gaps, so the mark no longer reads as a window pane at any size. The cost is that the lower-left region becomes a narrow wedge.
- `blob_small_tilt` is the best favicon so far: gaps 8 wide, visibly curved at 16 px, four regions separate, one amber. `blob_small` (untilted) is the one closest to the Windows layout and should not be used.
- Not yet done: choosing the final tilt angle (13 degrees was a first guess; 8 to 20 are worth comparing), fixing which region is amber, and drawing the wordmark.

## Blob shapes and two amber regions (2026-10-26)

`blob_small` (untilted, gaps 8, periods 16 and 20) is the preferred favicon base. `marks.py` now builds it for three outlines (`SHAPES`: `s0` the current one, `s1` wide and flat, `s2` rounder with a bay) with the left pair or the right pair of regions amber (`s0_left`, `s0_right`, ...). The figure rows are `s0_left`, `s0_right`, `s1_left`, `s1_right`, `s2_left`, `s2_right`.

- `s1` (wide) fails: the cut leaves a thin wedge in the lower left that vanishes below 32 px.
- `s0` and `s2` hold at 16 px. `s2` has the most country-like outline (a bay on the right, a bulge at the top).
- Two amber regions give a two-color mark whose halves read as a country split in two, with the gaps as a cross; amber on the left or right changes the weight of the mark (the right pair is larger in `s0` and `s2`, so amber on the right is the heavier choice). With two colors the Windows resemblance is, if anything, a little closer than with one amber region, because the four parts now read as two color groups; this is the thing to judge.

## Four different directions (2026-10-26)

Rendered in `fig1_marks.png` (rows `cmono`, `twist`, `rings`) and `fig2_wordmark.png` (wordmark at 400, 200 and 120 px wide). All four avoid the cross of four regions.

- `cmono` (a "c" of wavy concentric streamlines with an amber dot in the opening): does not work. The wobble reads as a hand-drawn pentagon and the "c" is only legible at large size; at 32 px it is a knot.
- `twist` (a 3 x 3 grid twisted around an amber centre cell): distinctive and holds its structure at 16 px, but it reads as a camera shutter or aperture, not a cartogram. A fixable reading, but a different association from the one wanted.
- `rings` (nested wobbly isolines around an amber core): the best of the four. It reads as a density field or contour map (the quantity the algorithm moves), holds at 32 px, and the one-color version is clean. Weak points: at 16 px the rings merge into a target, and the top left shows two rings touching, which should be separated.
- Wordmark (first draft, `carto-flow` in a rounded sans with a blue hyphen and an amber "o" in flow): the first draft had a wavy hyphen and a drawn "o"; the wavy hyphen collided with the "o" of carto and the spacing depended on fonts, so it was replaced by colored letters in live text. Fonts are not embedded, so the figure shows the fallback sans. A final wordmark needs a chosen, license-checked typeface with the letters converted to outlines.

## Rings, cf, velocity field, compared with s0 (2026-10-26)

`fig1_marks.png` rows: `rings2` (three rings), `rings_small` (two rings, stroke 7.5), `s0_right`, `cf` (streamline letters), `cf_solid`, `velocity` (radial arrows). `fig3_velocity.png`: `velocity2` (4 x 4 larger arrows from a source) and `velocity3` (uniform stream past an amber disc).

figure: fig3_velocity.png — The two larger-arrow velocity-field variants, at the same sizes.

- **Rings.** The rings no longer touch (the code asserts that no two stroked rings intersect). `rings_small` holds at 16 px as a bullseye with an amber core. The drawback that the refinement exposed: with the rings clean and nearly concentric, the mark reads as a target or an eye, not a density field. More drift between the ring centers (a hill with a steep side) would bring back the contour reading.
- **s0 (right pair amber).** Still the strongest at 16 px and the most recognizably cartographic, and the only one that shows regions. It keeps the flag and window association discussed before; the rings do not.
- **cf.** The streamline version (three parallel lines per stroke) is distinctive at 128 px but the offset curves of the hook and the crossbar collide with the stem and it turns to noise at 32 px. `cf_solid` is clean down to 16 px, with the crossbar of the f as the amber region; it reads as a rounded monogram, not as flow.
- **Velocity field.** Arrows only read at 64 px and above: at 32 px the heads vanish and at 16 px they are dots. The radial field (`velocity`, `velocity2`) reads as a burst or a sun. The stream past a disc (`velocity3`) reads as a flow, but the deflection is hardly visible with a 4 x 4 grid. Better suited as a large graphic (social preview, README header) than as the logo mark.

Ranking for the logo mark at all sizes: `s0_right`, `rings_small`, `cf_solid`; for large graphics: the velocity field.

## Taller, blobbier outlines with non-perpendicular gaps (2026-10-26)

`fig1_marks.png` now shows, from the top: `s0_right` (reference), `t0_v8_h-10`, `t0_v-10_h8`, `t1_v10_h-8`, `t1_v-8_h12`, `t2_v7_h-9`. The outlines `t0` to `t2` are taller than wide (width 0.78 to 0.85 of the height) with larger harmonics (amplitudes up to 0.12, plus a fifth or sixth harmonic). `vN_hM` rotates the vertical gap by N degrees and the horizontal gap by M degrees about the blob center, independently. For each combination `_best_cut` searches the crossing position for four pieces with the smallest piece as large as possible and a largest-to-smallest ratio of at least 1.6; amber is the two pieces with the largest centroid x.

- The two rows with a visible tilt difference (`t0_v8_h-10`, `t1_v10_h-8`) no longer read as a flag: the gaps are neither axis-aligned nor perpendicular, the pieces differ in shape, and the silhouette is a tall blob. `t1_v10_h-8` is the most blob-like and still shows four regions at 16 px.
- `t0_v-10_h8` and `t1_v-8_h12` end up nearly perpendicular (the two rotations partly cancel) and look like the earlier marks.
- `t2_v7_h-9` (the boxiest outline) has pointed lower-left and upper-left pieces that are a little aggressive at 32 px.

## Refining t1: crossing angle, gap width, contrast (2026-10-26)

figure: fig5_angles.png — `t1` outline with the gaps crossing at 90 (both rotated 1 degree), 80, 72 and 65 degrees (rows, top to bottom). The 58 degree crossing has no valid four-piece cut. Sizes 128, 64, 32 and 16 px, light and dark, one-color last.

figure: fig4_gaps.png — `t1` with the 72 degree crossing (vertical gap rotated 10 degrees, horizontal gap -8 degrees) and gap widths 6, 8, 10 and 12 (rows, top to bottom; units of the 96 x 96 viewBox).

- **Angle.** At 90 degrees the mark is still a cross; 80 degrees is close to it. 72 and 65 degrees read as a tall blob cut by two lines that meet at an angle. At 65 degrees the lower pieces become too unequal (a small lower-left piece), so 72 degrees is the choice: it is the least cross-like without losing a balanced set of pieces.
- **Gap width.** 6 is thin at 32 px and closes at 16 px. 8 holds down to 16 px. 10 keeps four separate regions at 16 px with more margin; at 12 the gaps start to eat the amber piece. Use 10 for the favicon and 8 for the mark at 32 px and above (6 for a hero-size version).
- **Contrast of the two colors.** Blue on the white surface 5.5:1, amber on white 2.9:1 (below the 3:1 of WCAG 1.4.11 for graphical objects), blue on the dark surface 7.8:1, amber on dark 10.9:1. Blue against amber, adjacent: 1.9:1 (light) and 1.4:1 (dark). The regions are separated by gaps, so the mark does not depend on that ratio, but the amber on white is slightly under 3:1; a darker amber for the light variant (for example #c27400) is worth testing.
