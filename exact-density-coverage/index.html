Inputs
US states (49 incl. DC) and congressional districts (432), bundled; MorphOptions defaults except where stated (grid 256, dt 0.2, recompute_on_rise 0.01, stall rule on); NUMBA_NUM_THREADS=8. True error is the output area against the population share of the total output area.

Each grid cell takes the area it covers of each region instead of the region at its center. Regions smaller than a cell take part, the coarse multiresolution level no longer plateaus, and rasterization is faster or equal from grid 256 up; accuracy at the stopping point is comparable, and far past convergence center sampling gets lower because its field goes exactly uniform. The default stays center.

The prototype and its first measurements are on exploration-area-coverage; this page measures the code of the PR. make_figures.py regenerates every figure and data/tables.md. Counties are not bundled and are not run here; the exploration page found them unchanged.

At matching tolerances the iteration counts are within 10 %, with one exception: the districts at 0.2 %/0.4 % stall at 220 iterations for exact coverage (true maximum error 0.49 %) where center converges at 235. The true errors at the stopping point are mixed: lower for exact on the states at grid 256 with default tolerances, the states at 512 at 1 % and the districts at 1 %, higher on the mean for the states at 256.

With center sampling the density field becomes exactly uniform once the rasterized regions match their targets, the velocity is zero, and the run freezes at a small true error. With exact coverage the residual never vanishes, and the normalized step (the fastest point moves dt cells) keeps moving the borders; the late-phase oscillation of #94. A smaller step near convergence is the lever for that, not the rasterizer. At realistic tolerances (above) both stop long before this matters. This is the reason the default stays "center".
